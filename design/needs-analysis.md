# Needs analysis

## The gap
- **Desired:** students build an argument from evidence about a planetary surface: observe, measure, compare, and state a claim with its limits.
- **Actual:** students meet planetary geology as pictures and facts to recall. Field work, the usual way to practice reading a landscape, is impossible for the Moon.

## Why a simulation, and why this site
- Virtual labs can support conceptual science learning (Frontiers in Education, 2026, cited in the shared standards). A site nobody can visit is the strongest case for one.
- The Apollo 15 site has public, high-resolution terrain (LROC NAC DTM, 2 m/px) and a well-documented human traverse, so learners can compare their reasoning with what the crew did.
- The real data contains a genuine teaching moment: crater counts on the steep Front come out *lower* than on the younger mare, because slopes erase craters. That makes "know your method's limits" part of the task.

## Constraints
- 45–60 minutes, in one or two sittings; progress saved locally.
- Mixed devices, including phones (Low preset).
- No automated grading of writing.
- Facts must come from opened sources; anything unverifiable is labeled or left out.

## Not addressed
Rover driving, real EVA physics, astronaut avatars and full lunar-globe navigation are out of scope (plan 01 §4).
