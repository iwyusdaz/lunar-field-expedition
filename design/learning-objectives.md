# Learning objectives

Each objective maps to practice, feedback and assessment in [alignment-matrix.md](alignment-matrix.md).

By the end of the course, the learner will be able to:

1. **Identify landforms and their likely origin.** Tag a sinuous rille, an impact crater, a mountain massif and a mare plain in real terrain, and describe how each likely formed.
2. **Explain how sun angle changes what relief you can see,** using the sun-angle tool (low sun casts long shadows that reveal relief; high sun flattens it).
3. **Compare relative ages** of the mare surface and the Apennine Front using superposition and crater density, and explain when crater counting can mislead (steep slopes erase craters).
4. **Plan a sampling traverse** that answers a stated question within EVA time and consumables limits, and justify each station choice.
5. **Write a claim-evidence-reasoning field report** that cites specific observations and measurements and names a limitation of the evidence.

Standards alignment (wording verified 2026-10-27, see `research/apollo15-facts.md`): NGSS HS-ESS1-6 (evidence from planetary surfaces, including the impact cratering record) and Science and Engineering Practice 7 (engaging in argument from evidence).
